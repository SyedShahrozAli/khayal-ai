Abstract
Pakistan faces a severe mental health crisis, with an estimated 24 million people requiring mental health services but only 0.19 psychiatrists per 100,000 population and just 0.4% of the health budget allocated to mental health. Stigma and limited access (mostly in big cities) further compound the issue. Khayal AI is conceived to address these gaps by providing a culturally sensitive, bilingual (English/Urdu) mobile and web application for emotional wellbeing support. It will offer guided journaling (text or speech), AI-driven sentiment analysis (including Urdu NLP), a safe AI-chatbot for stress relief, breathing exercises with animations, mini-games and a verified Pakistani therapist booking system. The project objectives include (BO-1) enabling accessible mental health self-care in Urdu/English, (BO-2) applying AI/NLP for emotional insight, (BO-3) offering immediate supportive chat during crises, (BO-4) fostering community peer support and (BO-5) improving access to vetted therapists. The expected impact is increased mental health awareness and support in Pakistan, lowering barriers to care and providing scalable first-line assistance.

Chapter 1: Introduction and Problem Definition
Introduction
Mental health issues like depression and anxiety are widespread globally, but low- and middle-income countries often lack adequate care. In Pakistan, traditional services are scarce, primarily hospital-based and concentrated in cities, leaving countless people underserved. Cultural stigma and lack of literacy in medical jargon prevent many from seeking help, even when resources exist. Digital health interventions such as chat-based therapy apps have shown promise elsewhere by providing anonymous, 24/7 support that feels non-judgmental. International apps like Woebot and Wysa use AI chatbots and self-care exercises to bridge therapist shortages. However, they cater mostly to English-speaking users and provide only basic support. There is a critical need for a locally relevant solution. Khayal will focus on Pakistani users’ language and cultural context, combining proven techniques (CBT journaling, breathing exercises) with Urdu-language support and community features to fill the local mental health support gap.
1.2 Problem Statement
Pakistan’s mental health system is overburdened and under-resourced. Only a tiny fraction of the population can access qualified therapy, and awareness of mental wellbeing is very low. Key problems include:
Scarcity of Professionals: With ~0.19 psychiatrists per 100,000 people, most sufferers go untreated.
Access Barriers: Services are urban-centric; rural and remote areas have virtually no mental health clinics.
Language & Literacy: Many Pakistanis are more comfortable in Urdu, but existing tools and content are predominantly English.
Stigma & Trust: Deep-rooted stigma means people avoid seeking help, fearing judgment. Digital anonymity can mitigate this, but no major platform has effectively localized mental health support.
Lack of Support Tools: Routine emotional self-care tools (journals, exercises, peer support) are not widely available or tailored to Pakistani culture.
Thus, Pakistan needs a solution that lowers these barriers through technology, enabling people to self manage stress and emotions and connect to help when needed, in a secure and culturally appropriate way.
1.3 Problem Solution/Objectives of the Proposed System
There is a critical need for a platform that addresses the scarcity of mental health resources in Pakistan. Khayal AI is a system that would permit users to anonymously access mental health services, defined as routine journaling, AI-driven chat support and structured care paths for conditions such as depression and anxiety, via a bilingual web and mobile interface.
Such a system would lower the barrier to entry for users fearing stigma, and it would provide an accessible, affordable digital companion for emotional well-being. The system would assess user inputs to identify potential mental health indications. Using AI for emotional analysis and content moderation would ensure safe community interactions and improve the accuracy of user evaluation. The ability to integrate a therapist booking system would bridge the gap between digital self-help and professional medical guidance, ensuring users are guided effectively based on their needs. In instances of critical severity where AI support is insufficient, the system would explicitly direct the user to consult a professional therapist.
1.4 Objectives
BO-1: Implement a dual-language journaling feature (English/Urdu, text/speech) with guided prompts, to help users express and reflect on feelings.BO-2: Develop an AI/NLP engine to analyze journal entries and chat interactions, detecting emotions and risk (such as high stress or crisis), with special support for Urdu language input.BO-3: Build a secure, scripted conversational chatbot that provides immediate coping strategies and emotional support during anxiety or stress, operating 24/7.BO-4: Include breathing exercises and simple mini-games (with calming animations) as distraction and relaxation tools to help manage momentary distress.BO-5: Implement a therapist booking module connecting users to verified mental health professionals (ensuring credibility), allowing online appointment scheduling.BO-6: Create a moderated community forum for peer support, where users can anonymously share experiences in Urdu or English.
1.5 Related System Analysis/Literature Review
We reviewed several existing mental health apps to understand their strengths and gaps:
Woebot: An early AI chatbot for mental health, Woebot used a CBT-based dialogue to guide users through mood tracking and coping exercise. About 1.5 million people used it, benefiting from its structured CBT conversation style. However, Woebot relied on pre-scripted responses and did not support Urdu; it has recently shut down due to regulatory and technological constraints. Khayal builds on this idea by offering chatbot support, but with a focus on Urdu language processing and cultural adaptation.
Wysa: This app serves as an “AI companion” offering text conversations and self-care modules. Wysa incorporates evidence-based techniques (CBT, breathing, mindfulness) and lets users journal thoughts, check mood, and join online discussions. Studies show users find it comforting and emotionally responsive, appreciating the non-judgmental space. However, some reviews note limitations in its AI understanding and a lack of personalization. Khayal will similarly offer CBT-informed journaling and chatbot guidance but will tailor the content for Pakistani culture and improve on Wysa by adding Urdu support and community networking.
Therapixie (concept): (Kovalenko et al., 2023) A mental health app prototype called Therapixie combines journaling with meditation and achievement tracking. It provides users with feedback on their entries and progress, increasing engagement. This concept underscores Khayal’s approach of blending reflective journaling with guided activities. Unlike Therapixie’s abstract design, Khayal is an actual system project that will implement such features and gather real user data.
Humraaz (Pakistan): Launched in 2023 by the government, Humraaz is a mobile/web app that connects citizens to psychologists/psychiatrists. It includes guided counselling sessions, daily mood monitoring, a chatbot for self-assessment, 24/7 access to professionals, and support groups. This is Pakistan’s first major mental health app. Khayal complements Humraaz by adding features not currently found there: emotionally intelligent journaling, Urdu-language NLP analysis, breathing exercises/games and a peer community. Khayal focuses more on ongoing self-care tools, whereas Humraaz emphasizes expert connections. In sum, Khayal fills the local gaps by providing an integrated platform with advanced AI moderation and support tailored to Pakistani users.

1.6 Vision Statement
For individuals seeking accessible mental health support and self-care, Khayal AI is a comprehensive digital companion that provides guided journaling, AI-powered emotional analysis, and conversational support, along with relaxation exercises and community resources. Unlike existing international solutions that often lack dynamic support or strictly focus on clinical booking, our product offers a culturally adaptive environment and a safe interaction space. This empowers users to manage stress and connect with help anonymously, ensuring support is both accessible and effective.
1.7 Scope
The Khayal AI project focuses on delivering a highly accessible and intuitive mental well-being platform. The scope includes a user-friendly AI Chatbot and Journaling system capable of processing bilingual input (English and Urdu) to maximize user inclusion, though a full interface translation is excluded.
Central to the scope is a safe, moderated AI chatbot designed to foster engagement while strictly filtering inappropriate content. The community module will be moderated to handle stigma and inappropriate content.  The system facilitates accessibility to professional care by linking users to a curated network of verified therapists. The application is scoped for standard web and mobile environments to ensure broad reach.
This app is not a substitute for professional psychiatric care; it is intended for mild-to-moderate stress and anxiety relief. We will not attempt to diagnose illnesses or provide emergency medical intervention. The app will run on standard smartphones and web browsers, assuming users have an internet connection.
Technically, the project focuses on optimizing AI responsiveness for emotional analysis and ensuring a stigma-free community environment. It explicitly excludes diagnostic capabilities, emergency medical intervention and support for regional languages beyond the core offering, positioning the platform as a tool for mild-to-moderate emotional support rather than clinical treatment.

1.9 System Limitations/Constraints
Urdu NLP Challenges: Limited existing lexicons and dialect variance mean sentiment analysis may be less accurate in Urdu than English. Data collection and model training for Urdu emotion analysis is non-trivial.
Therapist Availability: The number of qualified mental health professionals in Pakistan is low, so the booking module may have few options. Users may experience booking delays or limited slot availability. Onboarding foreign therapists will be considered.
Digital Divide: Khayal assumes smartphone or internet access. Rural users without internet or digital literacy are effectively out of scope.
Stigma and Privacy: Despite anonymity, users may hesitate to fully engage or reveal personal issues. Some user intents (e.g., self-harm) may not be perfectly detected, despite our best AI models.
Data Privacy & Security: Storing sensitive user data (journals, moods) demands strict encryption and safeguards; any breach would be critical. Implementing industry-standard security is complex but necessary.
Regulatory Constraints: The app is not a licensed medical device; we must clearly disclaim that we are not providing clinical diagnosis or therapy. Content must remain low risk (hence, scripted chatbot and admin oversight).
1.10 Data Gathering Approach
Focus Groups: Conduct group interviews with Pakistani university students and community members to identify common stressors, language preferences (Urdu vs English) and desired features.
Therapist Interviews: Meet with local psychologists/psychiatrists to understand therapeutic best practices, culturally appropriate journaling prompts and ethical considerations for a digital support tool.
Surveys: Distribute questionnaires (in both languages) to gather data on what users expect from a mental health app and any usability needs.
Urdu Dataset Building: Compile a corpus of Urdu text (from news comments, literature, social media) and annotate it for emotions or sentiments. This includes collecting Urdu speech samples to test speech-to-text accuracy.

2.1 User Classes and Characteristics
This section identifies the different groups of people who will interact with the Khayal AI platform. By defining classes such as Seekers (App Users), Care Providers (Therapists) and Admins, we can better understand the specific needs, technical skills of those who interact with the app. Understanding these characteristics ensures the app is accessible to everyone, from mental health help seekers to healthcare professionals.
User Class
Description
Mental health care seeker (Primary User)
Individuals seeking mental health support and emotional well-being. They will use the mobile app for journaling, mood tracking and interacting with the AI chatbot for stress relief. There is a high potential user base given the estimated 24 million people in Pakistan requiring mental health services. They are expected to use the app daily for self-care, with some transitioning to the therapist booking module for professional help.
Therapist
Verified Pakistani mental health professionals (psychologists and psychiatrists) who provide expert consultations. Technical staff or developers responsible for the operational integrity of the platform. Their job is to manage user accounts, verify and approve therapist credentials and oversee community moderation. They also monitor AI performance, specifically sentiment analysis accuracy.
Community Member
A subset of mental health help seekers who interact within the moderated peer support forums. These users post anonymously to share experiences and coping tips in a stigma free environment. Their interactions are subject to AI-driven moderation to filter harmful content and triggers.

OE-3: Geographical Location: Primary users are located across Pakistan, requiring the system to handle local network conditions and provide bilingual support (Urdu/English).
OE-5: Database and Storage: User journals, mood history, and chat logs shall be stored in Firebase Firestore and Firebase Storage, ensuring synchronization and secure data persistence.
OE-6: Hardware Integration: The system shall interact with smartwatches and smart rings to read body metrics (heart rate, sleep quality) for enhanced emotional analysis.
Design and Implementation Constraints
Constraints are the limitations that the development team must follow during the building process. For Khayal AI, these include using the Flutter framework for cross-platform mobile development and ensuring all AI responses follow strict safety protocols to prevent medical hallucinations. These boundaries help keep the project realistic, secure and focused on providing non-diagnostic support.
CON-1: The mobile application must be developed using the Flutter (Dart SDK 3+) framework. To ensure a single codebase can be deployed to both Android and iOS while maintaining high performance for animations like breathing exercises.
CON-2: All backend services and APIs must be built using Node.js (v18+) or FastAPI (0.110+). These environments are required to handle real-time chatbot logic and efficient routing for NLP.
CON-3: The system shall utilize Firebase Firestore as the primary database engine. Firestore is
required for real-time synchronization of chat logs and mood history, as well as handling secure user authentication via OTP.
CON-4: NLP models for emotion detection must be based on XLM-RoBERTa or BERT architecture. These models are necessary to provide the required bilingual support for Urdu-English sentiment analysis, which is a core part of the project.
CON-5: The application must use the Google Speech-to-Text API for voice journaling. This specific API is chosen because it offers the most reliable support for Urdu voice-to-text conversion.
CON-6: The Therapist Portal and Admin Dashboard must be developed using Next.js 14+ and Tailwind CSS. This ensures a responsive, fast-loading web interface for professionals to manage their schedules and for admins to moderate the community.
CON-8: The chatbot must utilise a scripted conversational flow with OpenAI GPT-4o-mini (for now, can be changed depending on suitability) as the underlying processing engine. Using a scripted approach with a restricted LLM is a safety constraint to prevent AI hallucinations and ensure the bot remains non-diagnostic.

2.8 External Interface Requirements
This section defines how Khayal AI communicates with its three primary user roles Seekers, Therapists, and Admins as well as all external hardware, software libraries, and communication services. It ensures seamless integration across the Flutter mobile client, the Next.js therapist/admin web portal, and the Node.js / FastAPI backend services defined in the Design and Implementation Constraints of this SRS.
User Interfaces Requirements
Khayal AI serves three distinct interface surfaces: (1) a Flutter mobile application for Seekers, (2) a Next.js web portal for Therapists, and (3) a Next.js Admin Dashboard. All surfaces must adhere to the standards below while respecting the bilingual (English / Urdu) nature of the platform as required by OE-3 and LOC-1.
2.8.1.1 Design Standards and Style Guide
DSS-1.1: The mobile application (Flutter) shall follow Material Design 3 (MD3) guidelines for   all component layout, spacing, touch behaviour, and motion.
DSS-1.2: The web portal and Admin Dashboard (Next.js + Tailwind CSS, as per CON-6) shall follow a consistent internal component library derived from Tailwind's design tokens to ensure visual parity with the mobile app.
DSS-1.3: The colour palette shall use clinically informed calming tones soft teals, muted greens, and off-white neutrals consistent with mental health application best practices (NFR USE-5).
DSS-1.4: Typography shall use Poppins (mobile) and Inter (web) as primary typefaces. Body text shall be set at 14–16sp (mobile) and 14–16px (web). All text must be stored in localization resource files with no hardcoded strings.
2.8.1.2 Screen Layout and Resolution Constraints
SLR-1.1: The mobile UI shall be fully responsive across screen sizes from 5 to 7.5 inches diagonal (COM-2) and must support both portrait and landscape orientations (COM-3).
SLR-1.2: The web portal shall be responsive from 1024px to 2560px viewport width. Below 1024px, the portal must display a prompt directing therapists to use the mobile-capable version when available.
SLR-1.3: Minimum touch target size shall be 44 × 44 points on all interactive elements.
SLR-1.4: RTL layout must be fully applied when the user switches to Urdu including text alignment, icon mirroring, and navigation direction without requiring an app restart
2.8.1.3 Persistent Navigation and Common Controls
PNC-1.1: A persistent bottom navigation bar shall appear on all authenticated Seeker screens, providing access to: Home, Chat, Community, Journal, Stats, and Settings. The bar must not be concealed by the soft keyboard or any overlay.
PNC-1.2: A Crisis Help button displaying verified Pakistani helpline numbers shall be permanently accessible within one tap from every screen in the Seeker interface, as per the Crisis Detection safety constraint (UC-13).
PNC-1.3: The home screen header shall display an unread notification badge that decrements in real time as notifications are read.
2.8.1.4 Accessibility Standards
ACC-1.1: The application shall conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA accessibility guidelines.
ACC-1.2: All images, illustrations, and non-text interactive elements shall include descriptive alt-text and accessibility labels readable by TalkBack and VoiceOver (ACC-2).
ACC-1.3: Colour must never be the sole means of conveying information. Form validation errors, mood states, and status indicators must use both colour and text/icon cues.
ACC-1.4: All custom components (mood emoji selector, animated breathing guide, avatar) must implement ARIA roles on the web portal and Flutter Semantics widgets on mobile.
2.8.1.5 Message Display Conventions
MDC-1.1: Success confirmations shall use a green toast at the bottom of the screen, auto-dismissing after 3 seconds. Example: 'Journal entry saved.
MDC-1.2: Inline validation errors shall be displayed in red directly below the failing input field in real time not on submission only (USE-4).
MDC-1.3: The Crisis Override UI shall present a full-screen non-dismissible overlay with a calming gradient, helpline numbers, and a 'Continue to App' button that becomes active after 5 seconds. It must appear within 2 seconds of crisis keyword detection.
MDC-1.4: Offline sync confirmation shall display an in-app banner notification 'Your draft has been synced' within 30 seconds of connectivity restoration (FR-E11, FR-O10.3).
Software interfaces
This section documents all external software components, third-party APIs, and backend services that Khayal AI integrates with. These interfaces correspond to the constraints defined in Section 2.4 and the functional requirements across Sections 2.7 and 2.8.
2.8.2.1 SI-1: OpenAI GPT-4o-mini AI Chatbot Engine (CON-8)
SI-1.1: The system shall use the OpenAI Chat Completions API (model: gpt-4o-mini, upgradeable) to process all user chat messages and generate AI therapist responses.
SI-1.2: Every API call shall include a controlled system-level prompt that enforces the chatbot persona, non-diagnostic boundaries, preferred AI tone (FR-O2.5), and crisis handling instructions.
SI-1.3: Conversation history limited to the last 20 message pairs shall be passed with each API request to maintain session context while controlling token usage.
SI-1.4: Responses shall be streamed via Server-Sent Events (SSE) and rendered incrementally in the chat bubble, displaying a typing indicator within 500ms of message submission (PER-1).
2.8.2.2 SI-2: Firebase Firestore Primary Database (CON-3, OE-5)
SI-2.1: The system shall use Firebase Firestore as the primary NoSQL database for storing all user accounts, mood check-in logs, journal entries, chat histories, appointment records, and community posts.
SI-2.2: Firestore real-time listeners shall power live UI updates for: mood history snapshots, notification badges, therapist schedule availability (FR-O7.3), and community reaction counts.
SI-2.4: Appointment slot booking (UC-20) shall use Firestore transactions to guarantee atomic slot reservation, preventing double-booking by concurrent users (FR-O5.4).
SI-2.6: The audit log collection shall be append-only with Firestore security rules preventing any deletion or modification of individual log documents (FR-E13).SI-3:  XLM-RoBERTa / BERT NLP Engine Sentiment & Moderation (CON-4)
SI-2.7: The system shall integrate an XLM-RoBERTa or BERT-based NLP model to perform bilingual (Urdu/English) sentiment analysis on all journal entries and chat messages.
SI-2.8: The same NLP pipeline shall power the AI pre-publication moderation engine, scanning all community posts and comments for harmful, stigmatising, or abusive language before publication (FR-E10). Scan must complete within 3 seconds
SI-2.9:  Guided journaling prompts shall be generated by querying the NLP engine's emotion embeddings against the user's recent mood history to produce contextually relevant prompts.
2.8.2.7 SI-3: Firebase Storage File Storage (OE-5)
SI-3.1: Firebase Storage shall be used for all user-generated binary content: profile pictures, therapist credential documents, and generated PDF progress reports.
SI-3.2: Uploaded credential documents (UC-26) shall be stored in a restricted bucket accessible only to the Admin service account and the uploading therapist.
SI-3.3: Profile images must be automatically resized by server-side to a maximum of 512 × 512 pixels. Supported upload formats are JPG and PNG only (FR-O2.2).
SI-3.4: Download URLs for all sensitive files (credentials, PDF reports) shall be pre-signed with a maximum expiry of 1 hour.
2.8.2.3 SI-4: Google Speech-to-Text API Voice Journaling (CON-5)
SI-4.1: The system shall exclusively use Google Cloud Speech-to-Text API for transcribing voice journal entries and voice chat messages.
SI-4.2: Audio recordings shall be transmitted to the STT API immediately upon the user tapping Stop. Transcription of a 1-minute recording must be returned and displayed within 8 seconds (PER-2, UC-08).
SI-4.3:  The STT API call shall specify both 'ur-PK' (Urdu Pakistan) and 'en-PK' (English Pakistan) as language codes, with automatic language detection enabled
SI-4.5: Original audio files shall be discarded immediately after successful transcription and must never be stored on device or server.
SI-4.6: Auto-stop shall trigger if the user has been silent for more than 10 consecutive seconds during a recording session.
2.8.2.4 SI-5: Firebase Authentication User Auth & OTP (CON-3)
SI-5.1: The system shall use Firebase Authentication to manage all user registration, OTP email/phone verification, JWT issuance, and password reset flows (FR-O1.1 through FR-O1.7).
SI-5.2: JWT access tokens shall expire after 60 minutes. Refresh tokens shall expire after 30 days. Silent token refresh must occur automatically before expiry (FR-E12, SEC-3).
SI-5.3: OTP codes generated during registration shall expire within 10 minutes. A new OTP may be requested after a 60-second cooldown (FR-O1.2).
SI-5.4: Password reset links shall be single-use and expire 30 minutes after generation. On successful reset, all active Firebase sessions for that account must be immediately revoked (FR-O1.7).
SI-5.5: Biometric authentication (fingerprint / Face ID) shall use the device's local biometric API (Android BiometricPrompt / iOS LocalAuthentication) and bind to the Firebase session token on success (FR-O1.9).
2.8.2.5 SI-6: Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) & Apple Push Notification Service (APNs) (FR-E7)
SI-6.1: All push notification payloads shall include a deep-link target so that tapping a notification routes the user directly to the relevant in-app screen (FR-O3.6).
SI-6.2: Notifications must be delivered regardless of app foreground, background, or closed state (FR-E7). For offline devices, FCM/APNs shall queue and retry delivery for up to 24 hours (UC-29).
SI-6.3: Appointment reminders shall be scheduled server-side and sent at exactly 24 hours and 1 hour before confirmed appointments, within a 5-minute tolerance. Reminders for cancelled appointments must be immediately de-scheduled (FR-E8, FR-O5.8).
SI-6.4: Crisis event silent Admin alerts (FR-O8.9) shall be transmitted as high-priority data messages (not visible notifications) via FCM to the Admin dashboard client.
2.8.2.6 SI-7:  Node.js / FastAPI Backend Services (CON-2)
SI-7.1: All business logic, NLP orchestration, AI API calls, scheduling, and data validation shall be handled by the backend API layer built on Node.js (v18+) or FastAPI (0.110+).
SI-7.2: The backend shall implement a strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) model at the API level. Any request to an endpoint outside the caller's role must return HTTP 403 and be recorded in the audit log (FR-O9.1).
SI-7.3: The backend shall expose a dedicated crisis keyword management endpoint, accessible only to Admin, that allows updating the crisis keyword list. Changes must propagate to the NLP scanning engine within 5 minutes (FR-O8.10).
2.8.2.7 AI Response Design
SI-1.8: The model must utilize XLM-RoBERTa or a similar Transformer architecture to handle cross-lingual embeddings.
SI-1.9: The AI is strictly prohibited from providing medical dosages or specific psychiatric diagnoses.
SI-1.10: All AI processing must be completed within 3 seconds to meet performance standards.
Hardware interfaces
Khayal AI is a software-only platform. It does not ship or control dedicated hardware. However, it interfaces with the hardware capabilities of user-owned devices as described below.
2.8.3.1 HI-1: Smartphone Hardware (Mobile Application)
HI-1.1: The Khayal AI mobile application shall operate on any Android smartphone running Android 10 (API Level 29) or higher and any iOS device running iOS 14 or higher, as specified in OE-1 and COM-1.
HI-1.2: The application shall interface with the device microphone exclusively for voice journaling (UC-08) and voice chat input Microphone access shall be requested using the platform's standard permission dialog; access shall be granted only with explicit user consent and shall not be retained when a session ends.
HI-1.3: The application shall interface with the device camera solely for profile picture upload (FR-O2.2), using the platform's native camera intent / UIImagePickerController. No camera stream shall be stored.
HI-1.4: The application shall use the device's biometric hardware (fingerprint sensor / Face ID) for the optional biometric login feature (FR-O1.9), accessed via Flutter's local_auth plugin. No biometric data shall ever leave the device.
HI-1.5: The application shall read the device clock and timezone for all timestamp display, mood check-in scheduling, and appointment reminder delivery (FR-O3.3, FR-E8).
2.8.3.2 HI-2: Smartwatch / Smart Ring Integration (OE-6)
HI-2.1: The system shall interface with connected smartwatches and smart rings via their companion SDK APIs (e.g., Samsung Health, Garmin Connect, or standard Bluetooth GATT profiles) to read passive biometric metrics: heart rate and sleep quality.
HI-2.2: Wearable integration shall be entirely optional. The application must function fully without a paired wearable device. The wearable pairing option shall be accessible from Settings.
HI-2.3: The system shall communicate with wearable devices exclusively over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). No wearable data shall be transmitted over the internet without explicit user consent.
2.8.3.3 HI-3: Server / Cloud Infrastructure
HI-3.1: The backend services shall be deployed on cloud infrastructure (Firebase-hosted services and containerised Node.js/FastAPI on a cloud provider such as Google Cloud Run or AWS ECS) and shall have no dependency on physical on-premises hardware.
HI-3.2: The infrastructure shall support the auto-scaling requirements defined in SCA-1 and SCA-2 to accommodate up to 100,000 registered users within 12 months of launch.
Communications interfaces
This section defines all network protocols, email delivery, real-time messaging, and data transmission standards used by Khayal AI.
2.8.4.1 CI-1:  Transport Security HTTPS / TLS
CI-1.1: All data exchanged between the Flutter mobile client, the Next.js web portal, and the backend API services shall be transmitted exclusively over HTTPS using TLS 1.2 or higher. HTTP connections shall return a 301 redirect to HTTPS (FR-E6, SEC-2).
CI-1.2: The mobile application shall implement SSL/TLS certificate pinning to prevent man-in-the-middle attacks. Any connection that fails certificate pinning shall be rejected and logged (FR-E6).
2.8.4.2 CI-2:  Offline Data Synchronisation
CI-2.1: When network connectivity is unavailable, the Flutter application shall use Firestore's built-in offline persistence to queue write operations (journal saves, mood check-ins) locally (FR-O10.2).
CI-2.2: Upon connectivity restoration, queued operations shall be automatically flushed to Firestore within 30 seconds. The user shall receive an in-app sync confirmation notification (FR-E11, FR-O10.3).
CI-2.3: In the event of a sync conflict (a draft modified both offline and online), the system shall present the user with a side-by-side conflict resolution screen. If unresolved within 7 days, the server version shall be retained (FR-O10.4).
2.8.4.3 CI-3:  Email Delivery Transactional Email Service
CI-3.1: The system shall use a transactional email service (e.g., SendGrid or Firebase Email Trigger Extension) to deliver all system-generated emails including OTP verification, password reset links, appointment confirmations, and therapist approval/rejection notifications.
CI-3.2: OTP verification emails shall be delivered within 60 seconds of registration. OTP codes expire after 10 minutes with a 60-second resend cooldown (FR-O1.2).
CI-3.3: Password reset emails shall contain a single-use link expiring in 30 minutes. The email must be delivered within 60 seconds of the request (FR-O1.7).
CI-3.4: Therapist approval and rejection emails (FR-E14) shall be sent within 60 seconds of Admin action and must include the stated reason for rejection where applicable.
2.8.4.5 CI-4:  Push Notifications FCM / APNs
CI-4.1: The system shall deliver all push notifications via Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) for Android and Apple Push Notification Service (APNs) for iOS, as specified in SI-6 and FR-E7.
CI-4.2:  Push notifications shall be localised in the user's selected language (English or Urdu) before dispatch (LOC-1, UC-29).
CI-4.3: Users shall be able to configure individual push notification categories on/off from Settings (FR-O3.2). Disabling a category must immediately de-schedule all pending notifications of that type.
2.8.4.6 CI-6:  Offline Data Synchronisation
CI-6.1: When network connectivity is unavailable, the Flutter application shall use Firestore's built-in offline persistence to queue write operations (journal saves, mood check-ins) locally (FR-O10.2).
CI-6.2: Upon connectivity restoration, queued operations shall be automatically flushed to Firestore within 30 seconds. The user shall receive an in-app sync confirmation notification (FR-E11, FR-O10.3).
CI-6.3: In the event of a sync conflict (a draft modified both offline and online), the system shall present the user with a side-by-side conflict resolution screen. If unresolved within 7 days, the server version shall be retained (FR-O10.4).
